About TEMPO, NO2, and this map

What is TEMPO?
NASA's TEMPO instrument observes air-pollution gases across North America every daylight hour from geostationary orbit. Its frequent measurements reveal how pollution changes during the day. NASA TEMPO mission

Where does NO2 come from?
Nitrogen dioxide is produced mainly when fuel burns in vehicles, power plants, aircraft, rail equipment, and industry. Spatial patterns and plumes can indicate which sources may be influencing an area, while winds can move those plumes away from their sources. NASA NO2 explanation

How is NO2 related to other pollutants? NO2 belongs to the NOx family (NO + NO2). In sunlight, NOx reacts with volatile organic compounds and contributes to ground-level ozone formation. How to view the latest TEMPO NO2

Use the official TEMPO public viewer to explore recent hourly NO2 observations for your location.

  1. Open the official TEMPO public data page.
  2. Enter your city or ZIP code in the search box.
  3. Select a date.
  4. Optionally select a featured topic such as traffic patterns, wildfires, or industrial sources.
  5. Drag the timeline marker to select an hour.
  6. Click Play to animate NO2 throughout the day.
